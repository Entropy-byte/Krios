## Method

- Each trail pixel becomes a sky direction through a level pinhole camera (elevation e, vertical field of view). One rotation axis (the pole) and one angular radius per arc are fitted on the sphere, so the projection's distortion of circles into ellipses is handled exactly.
- Pole altitude = altitude of the fitted axis; its uncertainty combines the fit, the camera-tilt reading (~0.3 deg) and the field-of-view error.
- Rotation rate = swept angle about the centre / exposure time; the sidereal prediction is 15.0411 deg/h.
- Prediction for the pole altitude: |latitude| (+ standard refraction); Polaris is NOT the pole - it sits ~0.7 deg away, so it is predicted separately.

## Assumptions and limits

- Camera level (no roll), rectilinear lens, field of view known to ~1 %, elevation read to ~0.3 deg.
- List the points of each trail in time order (first point = start of the exposure) so that the rotation sense is defined.
- A short arc fixes its circle poorly; use several arcs and 1-3 hour exposures.
- The flat comparison model is the strongest single-pole version: Polaris at a fitted height over the centre of a polar map.
