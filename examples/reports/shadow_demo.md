## Method

- Sun altitude h = atan(stick / shadow); uncertainty from both lengths plus a floor for the blurred shadow edge (the Sun's disc is 0.53 deg wide).
- Prediction: topocentric Sun from the Krios analytic ephemeris (~1"), oblate Earth, apparent altitude with standard refraction.
- Flat-map model (the strongest version): azimuthal-equidistant map centred on the north pole, Sun at height H above the sub-solar point (F1: H free) or above a free point of the map (F2: H, dx, dy free); tan h = H / rho.
- Eratosthenes: latitude of each station from its Sun altitude, the Sun's declination and the hour angle; angle between stations / measured ground distance = radius R.

## Assumptions and limits

- Clock error below ~10 s and longitudes accurate to ~0.01 deg (they set the hour angle).
- Level ground, stick truly vertical (plumb line); shadow tip read at the middle of the penumbra.
- Baselines must be measured independently (odometer, map, track length). If they are computed from the same coordinates the radius test is circular, and Krios will say so.
- The flat model is given free parameters on purpose, so it is not a straw man; a model that fits only by tuning is reported as such.
