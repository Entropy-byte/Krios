## Method

- Each sighting becomes a unit vector in an inertial frame (local alt/az -> Earth-fixed -> inertial with sidereal time); the observer's position vector comes from latitude, longitude and height on the WGS-84 ellipsoid.
- Moon position = least-squares closest point to all lines of sight; distance uncertainty from 3000 Monte-Carlo draws of the angular errors.
- Globe vs flat triangulation: globe = local verticals tilt with latitude/longitude; flat = a polar azimuthal-equidistant map with all verticals parallel. Statistic: miss distance of each line from the common point / (range x angular error).
- Time offsets inside a group are corrected with the predicted differential motion (a few arcseconds).

## Assumptions and limits

- Directions are APPARENT (include refraction) when given as alt/az; Krios removes standard refraction. RA/Dec from star matching need no refraction correction.
- Simultaneity within ~30 s and a clock good to ~1 s; azimuth measured from TRUE north (correct magnetic declination).
- Accuracy is set by the angular error and the baseline: 0.05 deg over 5000 km gives roughly 5 % in distance. Short baselines cannot measure the Moon.
- The flat map uses the common azimuthal-equidistant layout (111.195 km/deg); a flat-Earth advocate may supply another layout through station coordinates.
